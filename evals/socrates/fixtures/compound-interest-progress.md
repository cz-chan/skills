## Where you left off

You wanted to understand why compound interest grows money faster over time. You already found that each year's interest is calculated on the new total, not on the original 100 €. What's still open: what happens to that difference when you let it run for 30 years instead of 3?

## Target

Explain compound interest, apply it to a new case (a debt), and say when it stops helping you.

## Conclusions you reached

- Simple interest: 10 % of 100 € is 10 € every year, always the same.
- Compound interest: the second year's 10 % is calculated on 110 €, so it pays 11 €.
- The interest itself starts earning interest.

## Thinking errors and how you fixed them

- **Assumed:** 10 % a year for 3 years is always +30 % → **Realized:** with compounding it's 33.1 %, because each year's base is bigger.
